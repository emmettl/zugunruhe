# Potential avenues

Recorded 15 September 2026. These are possibilities for future studies, not a
committed implementation sequence. Continue to develop the visual language from
the observations; do not research migration visualisations or design precedents.

## A season of nights — first exploration underway

Extend the existing 37-station 2018 radar archive from individual nights to a
seasonal calendar. Preserve altitude structure, common density scales and gaps.
Explore spring/autumn comparison and moving from a seasonal overview into a
night. This describes one particular year, not a climatology or a population trend.

Source and existing interpretation: [First study](FIRST-STUDY.md).
New experiment: [Season of nights](SEASON-NIGHTS.md).

## Individual journeys

A small cohort of GPS-tagged birds could introduce continuity across departure,
travel, stopovers and return. Sparse accumulating paths could give pauses as much
weight as movement. A first CC0 subset is now verified and explored in
[A thread of passage](MIGRATION.md): fifteen white storks, July–September 2018.
Longer and more complete journeys remain an avenue to investigate. Study-specific download and
reuse terms apply. Connections between fixes must remain identified as
interpolation, and tagged individuals do not represent an entire population.

[Movebank data access](https://www.movebank.org/cms/movebank-content/access-data).

## The changing cast of birds

Species-specific weekly presence could become overlapping bands through the year
or geographic fields that expand and contract. eBird Status supplies modelled
weekly occurrence and relative abundance. Check coverage for each European species
before selection; population distributions do not establish routes or identify
species in the radar data. Downloads require registration and the documented
terms currently exclude commercial use.

[eBird data documentation](https://science.ebird.org/en/status-and-trends/faq).

## The landscape changes beneath them

Pair bird passage with recorded leaf emergence, flowering and autumn colour.
MeteoSwiss publishes plant-event dates at around 160 Swiss stations for 26 plant
species, with attribution required. A terrain study could reveal spring's timing
across elevations. Compare calendars without implying that one causes the other.

[MeteoSwiss phenological data](https://opendatadocs.meteoswiss.ch/a-data-groundbased/a9-phenological-observations).

For a broader region, consider Copernicus leaf area index and absorbed
photosynthetically active radiation at ten-day intervals. These describe vegetation,
not direct food availability. Check the selected product's quality flags, version,
period and licence before use.

[Copernicus vegetation data](https://cds.climate.copernicus.eu/datasets/satellite-lai-fapar?tab=documentation).
